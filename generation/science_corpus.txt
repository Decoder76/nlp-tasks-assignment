Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants use sunlight to synthesize food from carbon dioxide and water. This process occurs mainly in the leaves of plants and involves a green pigment called chlorophyll. The energy from sunlight is captured by chlorophyll and used to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose, a type of sugar, and oxygen, which is released into the air.

The human body consists of many systems that work together to maintain health. The circulatory system transports blood, nutrients, and oxygen to cells throughout the body. The nervous system transmits signals between different parts of the body and the brain. The respiratory system enables breathing, allowing oxygen to enter the lungs and carbon dioxide to be exhaled.

Electricity is a form of energy resulting from the movement of electrons. Conductors, such as copper, allow electricity to flow easily, while insulators, such as rubber, resist electrical flow. Magnets produce magnetic fields that attract or repel certain materials, and electromagnets can be created by wrapping a coil of wire around an iron core and passing a current through it.

Scientific research often involves forming hypotheses, conducting experiments, collecting data, and drawing conclusions. Measurement is crucial in science, with standard units used to ensure consistency and accuracy. Tools such as thermometers, microscopes, and scales assist scientists in observing and analyzing natural phenomena.
